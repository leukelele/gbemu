I'll try to keep it short for my future self's sanity but, in short, the
**Sharp LR35902** (based on the Z80) has eight 8-bit registers:
`A`, `B`, `C`, `E`, `F`, `H`, and `L`. These 8-bit registers are paired to
form: `AF`, `BC`, `DE`, and `HL`. I have represented the `register_file` as
follows:

```c
typedef union {
    struct {
        uint8_t lo;
        uint8_t hi;
    } bytes;
    uint16_t reg;
} reg_pair;
```

### why pair registers?

Immediately, this was the question that came to mind as I am someone who knows
naught of computing history and its early days (it may do well to some day
pick up a book on this topic). There a multiple advantages to pairing registers
but the most notable being that it opens the system to far greater
possibilities in expanding the addressable range from 2^8=256 to 2^16=65,536.

That is, pairing two 8-bit registers allows the CPU to represent larger values
without increasing the physical register width. This enables operations such as
addressing more memory, performing wider arithmetic, and handling counters or
pointers that exceed a single byte, all while retaining a simple 8-bit
architecture.

Such a simple but powerfull idea.
